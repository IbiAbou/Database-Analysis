
Rontec

* Leadership / ownership: Founder Sir Gerald Ronson remains Chairman/CEO and highly engaged at age 86; Giles Taylor is Managing Director. Tracy Moloney is the publicly identified senior finance executive, with the title Group Finance Director rather than CFO. The business remains controlled by Ronson, his family and associated family trusts. 
* Financing: Companies House security records identify Barclays as security agent under group financing arrangements, but no current facility quantum is publicly disclosed. Parent GMR Capital reported FY25 profit before tax of approximately £98m. 
* Commercial position: Approximately 267 sites. Its estate is extensively operated through third-party commission operators, while the proprietary shop’n drive format is present at more than 167 locations. 
* Back-pocket angle: Founder succession is the obvious strategic question, although Ronson has publicly said he is “not going anywhere.” Long-tenured lieutenants mitigate some key-person risk; Giles Taylor has worked with him for more than three decades. 

EG On The Move

* Leadership / ownership: Owned and led by CEO Zuber Issa; Rachael Hewitt is CFO. The UK business was carved out of EG Group in October 2024 for approximately £228m. 
* Financing: Rabobank provides working-capital facilities and HSBC has also announced debt support, although neither facility size has been made public. Issa has said that almost £500m of his own capital has been invested in the platform. 
* Commercial position: Reported more than £1bn revenue and £52m EBITDA for 2025. It acquired 98 Applegreen sites in 2025, 27 predominantly freehold MPK sites in May 2026 and Prax’s UK forecourt business in July. The UK network consequently reached roughly 285 locations. 
* Back-pocket angle: Currently an aggressive competing consolidator, not an obvious seller. The most relevant questions are integration bandwidth, leverage and whether external equity is introduced: Issa has indicated openness to private equity while retaining control. 

Ascona Group

* Leadership: Founder and CEO Darren Briggs; Shane Higgon is Group Finance Director. Duncan Morris leads property and Dave Beckett operations. 
* Financing: Completed a £200m funding package in late 2025, including £130m from Blazehill Capital and additional HSBC facilities. The package refinanced senior debt and includes undrawn acquisition capacity. This superseded or expanded upon a £92m HSBC/Barclays refinancing announced earlier in 2025. 
* Commercial position: Reached 70 sites following the acquisition of Truckhaven, a 17-acre M6 truck stop with 245 HGV spaces, hotel, restaurant and bar. Management has publicly targeted 120 locations. 
* Back-pocket angle: A well-funded buyer with a clear acquisition mandate. The diligence angle is whether its “Asconarisation” refurbishment model and central team can absorb rapid growth into more operationally complex assets such as truck stops. 

Penny on the Move

* Leadership / ownership: Founder and owner David Penny; Vicky Hennessy became CEO in 2025 and Martin Wheller was appointed CFO in September 2025. Tony Jackson is Chief Commercial Officer. 
* Financing: No current group facility quantum is publicly disclosed. Santander security is visible at operating-entity/property level, while the group has recently reorganised beneath Penny Forecourts Limited. 
* Commercial position: More than 90 sites and approximately 1,100 employees. The business is extending beyond conventional roadside retail: its 2025 acquisition of Barry’s Bakery added in-house food-to-go and bakery capabilities. 
* Back-pocket angle: The appointment of a CEO, CFO and CCO in quick succession suggests professionalisation ahead of the next growth phase—and potentially creates greater strategic optionality for the founder.

Park Garage Group

* Leadership / ownership: Three-generation family business chaired by Balraj Tandon MBE. Sunil Tandon is Managing Director, Hemant Tandon Operations Director and Manoj Tandon Finance & Technology Director. 
* Financing: No recent committed acquisition facility or quantum is publicly disclosed. Historic Companies House filings show Barclays security; the latest available financial data indicates a meaningful cash position rather than a heavily marketed leveraged-growth strategy. 
* Commercial position: More than 80 sites, substantially operated using the commission-operator model. It acquired three Hockenhull sites in 2025 and agreed a new wholesale arrangement with Co-op covering 80 stores in 2026. 
* Back-pocket angle: Relevant discussion points are generational succession, appetite for further acquisitions and the performance uplift expected from the Co-op retail reset.

Platinum Retail Group

* Leadership / ownership: Founded and controlled by Sej Sejpal; sons Jamie and Josh hold operating roles. No standalone CFO is publicly identified. Ricky Jhuti was recruited in September 2026 to lead the retail business. 
* Financing: Companies House records show both Lloyds and HSBC security. Management says it has bank funding in place to pursue a 100-site target, but the available facility and headroom have not been disclosed publicly. 
* Commercial position: Reached 60 sites after acquiring five Sterling Petroleum locations in January 2026. It has operated a commission-managed model across the estate since 2019 and continues to invest in sizeable site redevelopments. 
* Back-pocket angle: Another active competing buyer. The new retail leadership hire should support scale-up, but ownership and senior decision-making remain concentrated around the founder and family.

Karan Retail

* Leadership / ownership: Led by Managing Director Sivasubramaniam Suthakaran and co-owned with Kiriyagini Suthakaran. Visnu Kumaranisanthan is Operations Manager; no CFO is publicly identified. 
* Financing: Announced a new HSBC debt facility in 2026 to fund expansion. Companies House shows HSBC security across acquired properties, but the facility size has not been disclosed. 
* Commercial position: Reached 58 sites by June 2026; the subsequent Peebles acquisition implies approximately 59, including 15 Scottish locations. Recent growth includes a four-site Scottish portfolio and two further sites acquired in June. 
* Back-pocket angle: Strong acquisition momentum and fresh HSBC backing make Karan a credible auction competitor. The September departure of senior retail executive Ricky Jhuti to Platinum is worth probing from a management-depth perspective. 

NTS Retail

* Leadership / ownership: Founder and CEO Nalliah Thayaparan began as a commission operator in 1993 and established NTS in 2012. No CFO or finance director is publicly identified. 
* Financing: NatWest security is registered over group properties, but no group facility size or committed acquisition line is publicly available. 
* Commercial position: 28 sites, all operating under the commission-operator model, with a relatively lean central structure of four area managers and seven head-office staff. Management’s stated target is 35 sites. 
* Back-pocket angle: The uniform operating model should make bolt-ons relatively straightforward, but the lean central team and founder concentration create integration and key-person considerations.

The Kay Group

* Leadership / ownership: Owner and CEO Ken Kay; Richard Cox is Managing Director and Simon Fielding Finance Director. Ashley Kay, Ken’s daughter, leads business development and legal matters, providing visible next-generation management depth. 
* Financing: No current acquisition facility has been publicly announced. Historic NatWest security at holding-company level was satisfied in January 2025; this should not be interpreted as confirmation that the wider group is debt-free. 
* Commercial position: 24 developed, high-volume, 24-hour locations; construction has begun on the 25th site at Stafford, expected to open in 2027. The group has renewed its Booker/Londis supply partnership and is known for development-led assets, including the first Greggs drive-thru. 
* Back-pocket angle: A differentiated, quality-over-quantity estate. Any approach is likely to turn on valuation of freehold/development upside and the family’s longer-term succession intentions rather than immediate financing pressure.

Roadside Real Estate

* Leadership / ownership: AIM-listed. Charles Dickson is CEO, Douglas Benzie CFO and David Phillpot COO; Phillpot joined from BP, where he had overseen a 3,500-store European convenience business. 
* Financing: Raised £20.75m of equity and arranged £35m of HSBC capacity—£25m RCF plus £10m accordion. It also had a £25m Tarncourt facility controlled by Dickson, of which approximately £6.5m was drawn in March 2026; a separate £3.5m Barclays Gardner facility was subsequently repaid. 
* Commercial position: Built a 21-site platform through a rapid sequence of acquisitions, including Gardner, the £28.6m Hoch portfolio and the DAR forecourt and fuel-distribution business. 
* Back-pocket angle: A public-market roll-up with live access to both equity and debt capital. Key questions are acquisition integration, leverage headroom and governance around the CEO-controlled Tarncourt financing.

Pearl Forecourts

* Leadership / ownership: Controlled and led by Hanif Ahmed Mohmed. No separate CEO/CFO structure is publicly visible, underlining its owner-managed character. 
* Financing: NatWest holds security over multiple freehold properties, including the Bell Lane site; the underlying facility size is not public. 
* Commercial position: Opened its 19th site at Bell Lane, Bury, in October 2026 following an approximately £4m investment. Management has articulated a target of 25–30 high-quality sites. 
* Back-pocket angle: A property- and redevelopment-led operator willing to invest heavily per site. Founder dependence is high, but the stated 25–30-site ambition leaves room for acquisitions, funding partnerships or eventual consolidation.

Samy Group

* Leadership / ownership: Founded and controlled by Mohan Palanisamy. No group CFO is publicly identified, and the new holding-company structure has only limited consolidated reporting history. 
* Financing: Secured a £15m HSBC package in May 2026—described as the group’s first external investment—to fund acquisitions, consolidate intercompany loans and refurbish stores. 
* Commercial position: The financing announcement referenced 64 stores and a target of more than 100 by 2028; its website now states 73 convenience stores and forecourts. The forecourt subset stood at 16 in May 2026 and increased to 17 following its Cornish acquisition. 
* Back-pocket angle: Important to distinguish the total convenience estate from the actual forecourt estate. It is an active, newly bank-backed buyer, but financial reporting remains fragmented across entities and the owned-versus-commission-operated mix requires careful diligence.
